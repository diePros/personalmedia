# Local handwriting recognition (HTR) spike (roadmap spec §18)

Status: findings for the owner's go/no-go. Measurement only; nothing in `src/` changed. All data, models, venvs, scripts and raw results live in `$HOME/.cache/pms-htr-spike/` (not in the repo).

## Recommendation: GO WITH CONDITIONS

- **German: go.** Ship `naeyn/de-htr-web-v2` (ResNet18 + 3xBiLSTM + CTC, 10 M parameters, 40 MB fp32 ONNX, Apache-2.0) as the default `handwriting` engine. Measured on 10 unseen full German pages: **13.4% page CER, 34% WER, 71% of content words (len >= 4) recovered exactly**, about 0.6 s per page on the dev machine (est. 1.5 to 2.5 s on the N150), about 160 MB RSS in Python (est. 100 to 150 MB in Rust). It is a one-shot CTC model: no generation loop, no tokenizer. Ruling-wise it fits the 1.5 GiB container easily.
- **English: conditional.** The best local English model is `microsoft/trocr-small-handwritten` int8 (5.3% CER fp32, 6.8% int8 on IAM test lines, 82% content-word recall). It is 4x to 5x slower and 4x larger in memory than the CRNN, and its **page-level** performance on realistic photographed notes is **not proven** (see below). Make English a second engine, enabled only after the owner's own English pages pass a check (Decision 2).
- **Hard condition for both:** page segmentation works on clean binarised scans (projection profile: fine) but **fails on ruled notebook paper photographed with a phone** (GNHK sample pages: 64 to 85% page CER). A page-level product needs a better line finder (ruling removal plus colour-aware binarisation, or a small neural text-line detector) before it ships. Gate garbage by confidence and by engine/language match (below).
- Do not use Tesseract line boxes for handwriting (worse than projection profile in every test; section "Segmentation").
- **Caveats on the numbers:** no held-out German set exists that the German models have not trained on in some form; the page set used is the least contaminated one (see Data). The owner's own pages will be harder than these Wikipedia-text-on-plain-paper pages. Treat all CERs as best case.

## Setup

- Dev machine: Intel Core i5-11300H @ 3.10 GHz (4 cores / 8 threads, AVX2, AVX-512, AVX512-VNNI), 15 GB RAM. Other jobs (cargo tests, a crashed-and-restarted session) shared the CPU: load average was 4 to 6 during runs, so timings are pessimistic and noisy (thread scaling 2 vs 4 is not clean).
- Python 3.12 venv: `torch 2.15 (CPU)`, `transformers 4.57.6`, `optimum` (ONNX export), `onnxruntime 1.32.0`, `opencv-headless`, `rapidfuzz`. Tesseract 5.5.0 with `deu` (tessdata_fast) + `eng`.
- ONNX Runtime CPU provider, `intra_op_num_threads` = 2 or 4, `inter_op` = 1, batch size 1, greedy decoding (no beam search), KV cache on. One model at a time, one process per configuration; peak RSS = `/usr/bin/time -v` max RSS of that process (includes the Python interpreter, ONNX Runtime and, for TrOCR, `transformers`/`torch` imports, so it is an upper bound for a Rust `ort` process).
- Scripts (`$HOME/.cache/pms-htr-spike/scripts/`): `prep_data.py` (sample sets), `segment.py` (oracle / projection profile / Tesseract boxes), `quantize.py` (dynamic int8), `run_trocr.py`, `run_crnn.py` (recognition + timing), `driver.sh`, `driver2.sh` (all configurations, RSS capture), `aggregate.py` (CER / WER / recall / RSS tables), `gating.py` (confidence gating), `common.py` (metrics). Raw per-sample results: `out/*.json`, `out/summary.json`, `out/rss/*.txt`.

## Method

- **Line-level**: 60 German and 60 English line images, each recognised alone. CER/WER = micro-averaged Levenshtein (NFC, whitespace-collapsed). Seconds per line = mean of the model call only (image decode excluded), after one warm-up call.
- **Page-level**: segment page into line crops (below), recognise each crop, join with newline; page CER/WER on whitespace-collapsed text against the page ground truth. (Page set A's ground truth only covers annotated lines, about 90% of the content, so extra recognised text counts as errors; B has complete ground truth.)
- **Search usefulness**: recall = fraction of ground-truth content words (letters only, lower-cased, length >= 4) that appear exactly in the recognised text (set membership). Precision = fraction of recognised content words that are in the ground truth (garbage measure).
- **Segmentation**:
  - *profile*: Otsu/adaptive binarise, remove long horizontal/vertical rules by morphological opening, drop specks, row profile smoothed, line pitch from autocorrelation, line centres = local maxima at least 0.6 pitch apart, cut at profile minima, crop to the ink's horizontal extent plus 6 px padding.
  - *oracle*: annotated line boxes (set A only), the upper bound for segmentation.
  - *tess*: `tesseract -l deu+eng --psm 6` (and 3) TSV, union of word boxes per (block, paragraph, line), expanded by 25% above and 50% in height (*tess*) or used raw (*tessraw*).

Int8 quality loss: CRNN none (+/-0.1 pt); TrOCR-small-de +0.5 pt CER; TrOCR-small-en +1.5 pt CER (5.3 to 6.8%), about 3 pt recall. (Python RSS contains about 100 to 150 MB of interpreter/ORT overhead, so a Rust process will be lower.)

TrOCR-small-de is worse than the CRNN on ScaDS lines by a wide margin (22.7% vs 4.3% CER, although the CRNN's number is flattered by leakage): it generalises poorly to unseen writers and texts, and its decoder invents plausible German words. I would not ship it.

How segmentation errors move page CER (observed):
- Profile segmentation costs nothing on these clean scans versus oracle boxes (set A: 20.9% vs 19.9% CER, 71.6% vs 58.5% recall; profile recall is higher because the oracle only covers annotated lines).
- Tesseract's layout analysis is built for printed text; on handwriting it merges or splits lines (on one page it found a single line). Page CER roughly doubles (13% to 24 to 27% with tight boxes) and content recall falls from 71% to about 53%. (The dev box had only `eng` installed; I downloaded `deu` tessdata_fast for the test.) Verdict: do not use it for HTR line finding.
- Page images matter more than boxes: the same CRNN gets 4.3% CER on the provided clean line crops but about 20% on crops cut from the (strongly thresholded, lower-resolution) page images of set A, even with oracle boxes. The on-disk representation the pipeline gives the HTR stage (resolution, binarisation) is a first-order quality factor. Feed it the original colour/grayscale scan, never an already thresholded copy.

English pages (GNHK, 5 photographed notebook pages, 2 to 18 lines each; int8 TrOCR-small-en): **64 to 85% page CER, 35 to 46% content recall** with either segmenter (best: tessraw, 69.7% CER, 46.5% recall, 66% precision). CRNN-de and TrOCR-small-de on the same pages: 66 to 74% CER, recall 4 to 11%. Causes seen: ruled paper (rules survive binarisation and merge lines), coloured ink and perspective, printed text mixed in (GNHK ground truth excludes it, so the model's correct reading counts as an insertion), and the model inventing text (e.g. "Help Learn to edit Community portal Recent changes Upload file", a Wikipedia boilerplate hallucination) on blank or non-text crops. These pages are a stress test, but ruled-paper phone photos are plausibly the owner's real input, so this is the main product risk.

### Confidence gating

TrOCR (mean token probability of the greedy path per line): separates good from bad lines well.
- English IAM lines, `en_small_int8`: conf >= 0.5 keeps 95% of lines at 5.9% CER; >= 0.7 keeps 88% at 4.5%; >= 0.8 keeps 70% at 2.6%.
- Hallucinated crops on GNHK pages have conf 0.10 to 0.25; real lines 0.5 to 0.8. Page content precision at conf >= 0.3 / 0.5 on the GNHK stress pages: 44% to 50% (from 27% ungated, profile segmentation) and 73% to 80% (from 66%, tessraw), at the cost of dropping 33 to 71% of the lines.
- TrOCR-small-de on ScaDS lines: conf >= 0.7 keeps 93% at 20.8% CER; >= 0.8 keeps 55% at 13.5%.

CRNN (geometric mean of per-character softmax over emitted characters): **overconfident**, weak gate.
- German in-domain: conf >= 0.8 keeps 98% of lines at 3.6% CER; >= 0.9 keeps 93% at 2.7%.
- It stays confident on text it cannot read: on English lines the mean confidence is about 0.9 at 18% CER (conf >= 0.9 still keeps 73% of the lines at 15% CER). Set B pages: conf >= 0.8 drops 9% of lines, content precision 68.6 to 71.3%, recall unchanged; >= 0.9 drops 26% of the lines of the A pages and recall falls 71 to 64%.

So gating alone cannot catch "wrong language / not handwriting" for the CRNN; the engine must be matched to the document language, and a document-level check is needed (below).

### N150 estimate

Assumption: the N150 is about 2.5x slower than this dev core for a single thread (range 2 to 3x; the earlier embedding spike measured 1.46 to 1.69x by benchmark ratio but warned that the N150 has no AVX-512/VNNI, and fp32 conv/LSTM is not VNNI-bound anyway), and the dev timings above were taken at load average 4 to 6, so they are upper bounds. Estimated page = 15 lines; 4 and 2 threads shown as ranges.

| Engine | Dev s/page (4 / 2 thr) | N150 s/page (est., 2.5x) | 20-page document | Memory in a Rust `ort` process (est.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CRNN-de fp32 | 0.6 / 1.0 | **1.5 to 2.5** | 30 to 50 s | 100 to 150 MB (40 MB model, activations small) |
| TrOCR-small int8 | 3.0 / 2.7 | **7 to 8** (range 6 to 10) | 2.3 to 2.7 min | 300 to 450 MB (65 MB int8 weights + KV cache + 384x384 ViT activations); the Python process measured 600 MB |
| TrOCR-small fp32 | 3.9 / 4.9 | 10 to 12 | 3 to 4 min | 600 to 700 MB: do not |

Together with the embedding model (about 450 to 510 MB RSS) and the server, the CRNN fits the 1.5 GiB container comfortably; TrOCR-small int8 fits only because HTR runs one job at a time, after OCR and embedding, and the model unloads when idle. Page-level work is a background job (minutes per document is acceptable), so latency is not the blocker; memory and quality are.

## Risks and Rust (`ort =2.0.0-rc.15`) port notes

- **CRNN port is easy**: load ONNX, resize to height 64 (width capped at 1024, pad to a multiple of 4 with white), `f32` in [0,1], one `run`, softmax/argmax, collapse repeats, drop blank (index 0), map through the 111-character alphabet in `config.json`. No tokenizer, no loop, no custom op. About 150 lines of Rust plus image handling (`image`/`imageproc`).
- **TrOCR port is moderate**: the merged decoder needs a hand-written greedy loop with the `use_cache_branch` flag and 6 layers x 4 past-key/value tensors passed back each step; the 384x384 image preprocessing (bilinear resize, mean/std 0.5), the 64k sentencepiece vocabulary (`tokenizer.json` works with the `tokenizers` crate, decode only), and EOS/max-token handling. No custom ops; the optimum export validated within 5e-4. Beam search is not needed (greedy was measured).
- **Segmentation is the real engineering**: profile segmentation is about 100 lines but fails on ruled paper, skew and photos; a robust line finder is the biggest piece of work and the biggest quality lever. Everything beyond clean scans needs deskew, ruling removal and adaptive binarisation, and probably a small neural text-line detector (not evaluated here).
- **Hallucination risk**: TrOCR invents fluent text on blank or non-handwritten crops. Gate per line (conf >= 0.5) and per document (below); never index below the gate (`text_source = 'none'`).
- **Wrong-language risk**: the CRNN yields confident garbage on English; TrOCR-small-en yields garbage on German (33% CER, 13.9% recall int8). The engine must follow the document language, with a fallback to no HTR rather than the wrong engine.
- **Leakage / measurement risk**: see Data. The real accuracy on the owner's pages is unknown until measured.
- **Writer dependence**: published per-writer CER for the CRNN is heavy-tailed (median 6 to 8%, worst writers above 30%). Expect some pages to be unusable.
- **Licence housekeeping**: attribution for ScaDS (CC BY 4.0) is requested in the CRNN's data NOTICE; carry it in the licence page. TrOCR-small-handwritten has no licence tag (confirm); `fhswf`-derived weights are AFL-3.0 (permissive). IAM data itself is non-commercial and was used only for evaluation here.
- **Supply chain**: both recommended models are from individual or institutional HF accounts; pin revisions and SHA-256 like the embedding models (`fetch-model` pattern). The CRNN files and `config.json` are small enough to also vendor into the build stage for tests.

## Suggested defaults

- **Default engine**: CRNN-de fp32 (`model-fp32.onnx`, not int8). The int8 file is smaller but slower and no more accurate.
- **Optional English engine**: TrOCR-small-en int8 (encoder + merged decoder, about 63 MB), enabled only if the owner's English check passes.
- **Line gate**: CRNN: drop lines with confidence < 0.80; TrOCR: drop lines with mean token probability < 0.50. (On set B this removes about 9% of CRNN lines at no recall cost, 5% of TrOCR lines on IAM.)
- **Document gate** (write `text_source = 'htr'` only if all hold): at least 60% of the segmented lines survive the line gate; mean confidence of surviving lines >= 0.90 (CRNN) or >= 0.65 (TrOCR); at least 20 recognised content words; the recognised words pass a language check (e.g. at least 30% of recognised tokens are known words of the target language; this is what catches the CRNN on English, where confidence does not). Otherwise keep `text_source = 'none'`.
- **Never overwrite** `transcription` text; store the per-document mean confidence for the UI hint.
- Search: because content-word recall is about 70% (the CRNN, German pages), index HTR text with the same chunking, but flag it as low-trust in ranking and prefer fuzzy/prefix matching (e.g. trigram or edit-distance 1 on words of length >= 5), since most misses are single-character errors.

## Decisions for the owner

1. **German first?** Go with the 40 MB CRNN-de as the v1.6 engine (about 70% of content words findable on clean modern German pages)? Or wait until it is validated on the owner's real German pages (recommended before shipping: provide 10 pages as photographed/scanned in normal use; the same scripts rerun in about 5 minutes).
2. **English**: accept a second, heavier engine (TrOCR-small, +63 MB model, +300 to 450 MB RSS while running, 4x slower), or ship German only and treat English handwriting as AI-transcription only. English page quality is unproven; the line-level quality (5 to 7% CER) is good.
3. **Licence check**: confirm the MIT status of `microsoft/trocr-small-handwritten` (no tag on the card) before bundling.
4. **Input pipeline**: HTR needs the original scan/photo (grayscale or colour, >= 150 dpi equivalent), not the thresholded copy. Ruled-paper phone photos need a line-removal/segmentation step that does not exist yet; is a (larger) segmentation effort acceptable in v1.6, or limit v1.6 to clean scans?
5. **Fuzzy search**: add typo-tolerant matching for HTR text, or accept 70% recall for exact words?
